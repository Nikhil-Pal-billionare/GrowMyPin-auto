Pinterest is a visual search engine, not a social feed

The first thing we teach every seller is that Pinterest works very differently from Instagram or Facebook. People do not open Pinterest to see what their friends are doing. They open it to search for ideas: gift ideas for mom, living room decor, engagement ring styles, easy crochet patterns, a healthy morning routine. Pinterest behaves like a search engine with pictures, and that changes how marketing on it should be done.

Because people search, a good pin can be found again and again for a long time. A post on a social feed is seen for a day and then buried. A pin that answers a real search keeps getting shown as long as people keep searching for that idea. Our whole method is built on this one fact. We do not chase likes or followers. We create content that matches what buyers search for and makes them want to click through to your store.

We start with the buyer, not the product

Most sellers describe their products the way they make them: the material, the size, the technique. Buyers search in a completely different way. They search for the moment, the person or the feeling: a gift for a new mom, a cozy reading corner, a minimalist everyday bracelet, a statement rug for an entryway. So before we write anything, we ask who buys this product, who it is for, what occasion it fits and what problem it solves.

From these answers we build a small set of buyer angles for every product. One product can serve a gift buyer, a home decorator, a bride to be and someone treating themselves, and each of them needs a slightly different pin. This buyer first thinking is what separates pins that get views from pins that get clicks. When a pin speaks directly to the person searching, they feel it was made for them, and they tap.

Keyword research the way real buyers type

Every strong pin starts with the right words. We research the phrases buyers actually use on Pinterest by studying search suggestions, related topics, rising trends and the language used on popular pins in your niche. We look for phrases that show intent, like gift ideas, outfit ideas, room decor ideas or pattern for beginners, because those searches come from people who are ready to act.

Then we use those words naturally. A pin title should read like a helpful headline, not a list of tags. One clear topic per pin, written in simple language, works far better than a title stuffed with every keyword we can think of. Keyword stuffing confuses Pinterest about what the pin is really about, and it makes the pin look untrustworthy to people. Clear, natural wording helps the algorithm and the human at the same time.

Seasonal planning that starts weeks ahead

Pinterest users plan early. People start saving holiday decor, gift ideas and party themes well before the day arrives, and Pinterest begins showing seasonal content to more people as that interest builds. If your seasonal pins go live at the last minute, the biggest wave has already passed. That is why our planning always runs ahead of the calendar, so your content is ready and settled before the search peak begins.

We follow the natural rhythm of the year: the build up to the winter holidays, the gift seasons around birthdays, weddings and celebrations, the fresh start energy of the new year, and the home and lifestyle refresh that comes with each new season. Seasonal pins bring fast waves of attention, while evergreen pins keep a steady flow going all year. A healthy account needs both, and we plan the mix for each shop based on what it sells.

Pin designs that stop the scroll

On Pinterest, the image is the first and most important message. A plain product photo on a white background rarely stands out in a busy feed. We design pins that show your product inside a beautiful, believable scene: jewelry on a hand in soft natural light, a rug in a bright and elegant room, a crochet toy next to a cozy blanket, a skincare product on a calm morning vanity. Good light, real shadows and warm colours make people pause.

Every design is made for the way people browse. Pins are vertical so they take up more space on a phone screen, text is short and large enough to read at a glance, and the layout guides the eye to the product. We test different visual styles for each shop, from clean minimal designs to rich lifestyle scenes and bold text pins, and keep the styles that earn the most clicks. Design is not decoration for us. It is the reason someone chooses your pin over the one next to it.

Many pin angles for one product

Showing the same product the same way again and again leads nowhere. Instead, we create several different angles for each product, and each angle reaches a different kind of searcher. A gift angle speaks to people looking for a present for someone special. A seasonal angle ties the product to an upcoming holiday or event. A curiosity angle uses a short hook that makes people want to know more. A lifestyle angle shows the product as part of a dream room, outfit or routine.

There are also helpful angles, such as styling ideas, care tips or simple how to guides, that give value first and introduce the product naturally. Using many angles means one product can appear in many different searches without repeating itself, and it gives us real information about which message works best for your audience. Over time, the strongest angles become the backbone of your content plan.

Titles, descriptions and alt text written for people

Once the design is ready, the words have to do their job. A strong pin title tells the searcher exactly what they will find and why it matters to them. We keep titles clear, specific and benefit focused, and we make sure the main search phrase appears in a natural way. Descriptions add context in a few short sentences: who the product is for, what makes it special and what the person can do next.

We also write alt text for every pin. Alt text describes the image for people who use screen readers, and it gives Pinterest one more clear signal about what the pin shows. Throughout all of this, we keep the tone honest. No fake discounts, no exaggerated claims and no misleading promises. Pins that tell the truth build trust with buyers, and trust is what turns a click into a sale.

Inspiration pins and product pins working together

Not every pin needs to sell directly. Some of the best performing content on Pinterest is pure inspiration: a beautiful room, a styling idea, a list of gift ideas, a helpful tip. These pins attract saves and help Pinterest learn what your account is about and who loves your content. They build reach and reputation, which makes it easier for your product pins to be shown to the right people.

Product pins are the ones that send buyers straight to your listings. They show a specific product clearly and give a strong reason to click. We plan both types together so they support each other, and we adjust the balance for each account as we see how its audience responds. An account that only sells feels like an advert, and an account that only inspires never earns a visit. The right mix does both.

Blog posts that add Google traffic

Pinterest is powerful, but it is even stronger with a blog behind it. We write blog posts around the topics your buyers care about: gift guides, styling ideas, room makeovers, care and cleaning guides, pattern tips and seasonal ideas. Each post is structured for search engines with clear headings and helpful answers, so it can be found on Google as well as through your pins.

Inside every post, your products are presented naturally with links to the listings, so readers can go from an idea to a purchase in one tap. Pins can send people to the blog, and the blog sends people to your shop. This creates a second traffic channel that keeps growing as more posts are added, and it gives buyers more reasons to trust your brand before they buy.

Boards that tell Pinterest what you are about

Boards are more than folders. They are one of the clearest signals Pinterest uses to understand your account. We organise boards around real search topics in your niche, give them clear names and descriptions, and save each pin to the board where it fits best. A jewelry shop might have boards for engagement rings, minimalist everyday jewelry and gifts for her, rather than one large board called Shop.

Good board structure also helps buyers. When someone lands on your profile, clear boards make it easy for them to explore more of your products in the style they like. A well organised profile looks like a real brand that knows its customers, and that impression matters when someone is deciding whether to trust a shop they have just discovered.

Original visuals, never copied content

One of the fastest ways to make an account invisible on Pinterest is to use images that already exist elsewhere, for example product photos copied from another seller or reused stock images. Pinterest can recognise duplicate content, and accounts that rely on it often see their reach quietly disappear. That is why every visual we create is made fresh for your product and your account.

When we use AI tools to help create lifestyle scenes, we use them to present your real product in a new setting, not to invent features your product does not have. Your buyer should receive exactly what they saw in the pin. Fresh, original and honest visuals keep your account healthy, protect your reputation and give Pinterest a reason to keep showing your content to new people.

Consistency beats bursts

Pinterest rewards accounts that show up regularly. Publishing a large number of pins on one day and nothing for the next two weeks looks unnatural and rarely works well. We publish on a steady rhythm that matches a healthy brand account, so Pinterest sees a reliable source of fresh content and keeps testing your pins with new audiences.

Steady publishing also protects your account. Sudden bulk posting, repeated identical pins and spam like patterns can trigger limits or reduce distribution. Our schedule is planned in advance, approved by you and spread out naturally over time. It is quieter than a burst of activity, but it builds a far stronger and safer account in the long run.

Measuring what matters: clicks and store visits

Pinterest shows many numbers, and not all of them matter equally. Impressions tell us how often pins are shown. Saves tell us people like the idea enough to keep it. Outbound clicks tell us people cared enough to visit your store, and that is the number we care about most. We read all of these signals together to understand what is working and what needs to change.

Every month we use these insights to improve the plan. We create more of what earns clicks, refresh designs that are not performing, retire topics that have faded and prepare for the next season early. This learning loop is what turns a good start into strong, steady growth. Marketing is never finished, and the accounts that keep improving are the ones that keep winning.

What we never do

Some promises are just as important as the work itself. These are the things GrowMyPin will never do with your account, because they put your shop at risk or break the trust of your buyers.

  • No fake engagement. We never buy followers, saves or clicks, and we never use bots.
  • No posting without approval. Every pin waits for your yes.
  • No keyword stuffing. Pins are written for people first.
  • No copied images. Every visual is made fresh for your product.
  • No misleading claims. No fake discounts and no promises your product cannot keep.
  • No password sharing. Pinterest is connected through the official sign in.

Frequently asked questions

Is Pinterest good for Etsy and Shopify sellers?

Yes. Pinterest is a visual search engine where people plan purchases, save gift ideas and look for inspiration, which makes it a natural fit for products like jewelry, home decor, crafts, wellness and gifts.

Do you use hashtags or lots of keywords?

We use natural search phrases in titles and descriptions and keep every pin focused on one clear topic. Stuffing many keywords into a pin usually hurts more than it helps.

Do you create new images for every pin?

Yes. We create fresh visuals for your products instead of reusing copied or duplicate images, which keeps your account healthy on Pinterest.

Why do you also write blog posts?

Blog posts can rank on Google and give your pins a helpful destination with links to your products. Together, Pinterest and blog content create two paths to your store.

How do you decide what to post each month?

We combine your buyer angles, upcoming seasons, rising search trends and the results of your past pins, then build a plan that you review and approve.
